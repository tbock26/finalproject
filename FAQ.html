Frequently Asked Questions

I don’t have an arts background. Where’s a good place to start teaching my kid about art?

  • Open Studios’ online program is an excellent hands-off way of getting your kid started with arts education. We recommend beginning with either creating your own artwork on our painting page, or learning about historical paintings on our art history page. Once your kid does these, there’s a quiz that they can work on to test their skills. We also recommend doing these lessons with your kid! Learning about art together is a great way to connect, and you may learn a thing or two yourself.

I’m worried about my kid’s screen time. Do you have recommendations for arts learning offline?

  • Absolutely! While the starting section of our site is primarily online activities, you can find in-class and at-home activities on our website as well. This allows your student to engage with some of the artists in our student module by making their own, hands-on projects offline!

How do you source the images from your site? Where can we see them in real life?

  • We have used images from the Art Institute of Chicago’s open source API. All of these paintings are held in the Art Institute of Chicago’s collection. Many of them may be on view, but you can check out their website in advance, since some paintings may be in storage or travelling for other exhibitions.

My kid excels at art! Can we use activities outside of the specified age range?

  • You can definitely level up! Since our activities are designed around artists and their techniques, there is something to be gained from doing any level of project. You can definitely encourage your kid to do a higher-level project if they are breezing through the others. Alternatively, it can be fun for kids to do the same project, but bigger! A bigger canvas allows them to play with scale and composition in new, interesting ways.

My kid needs a little more support. Can we use activities for younger students?

  • Of course! Our activities are designed for certain age groups, but can easily be modified according to your child’s needs. If you choose to use an activity for a younger category, you can easily increase the challenge slightly by creating a more intricate artwork with the simpler technique. Alternatively, you can use simpler subject matter with more complicated techniques if that suits your child’s skills better. These activities are great jumping off points, and can be modified however you need!
